GuaiMei Quanta: Limits of Mathematical Truth

Strength: Clear, correct, and concise. She explains undecidability accurately in two sentences.
Growth area: This reads like a textbook definition. Compared to TePi, who immediately saw the human implication (“this can encourage people stuck in life”), GuaiMei stays in the safe zone of summarizing. She should be pushed: “Why should a 12-year-old care about this?”
Q2 — How They Did It
When you use a Diophantine equation, the values of the solution are a sequence/set. You can use a computer program to calculate this sequence. A Turing machine is one such computer program.

Growth area: Like W1, she gives the right answer but stops short. She doesn’t explain the implication: if equations = Turing machines, and the halting problem is undecidable for Turing machines, then… She has the pieces but doesn’t connect the final dot. Pattern confirmed: she states facts accurately but doesn’t build arguments from them.

This statement is not true. Specifically the part about “It doesn’t matter who you are or what you are”. There is somebody who knows all things, and that is God. He knows all things, so he knows the answer, the solution.
Notable: This is a remarkable response.
